================================================================================
STORY IMAGES — what to generate, and where to put them
For the "How it works" rail on the homepage.
================================================================================

WHAT CLAUDE CAN AND CANNOT DO
  Claude wrote these prompts but CANNOT generate images — there is no image tool
  in the session, only text and files. Turning a prompt into a .jpg is the part
  that needs you.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Open any image generator — ChatGPT, Gemini, Midjourney, whichever you have.

  2. Paste ONE prompt below. Generate. Download the image.
     Repeat for each. Generate all of them in the SAME tool in the SAME sitting —
     switching tools halfway will show.

  3. Save each file, with these exact names, into:

        app/vthacks-career-app/public/collage/

     Full path:
        /Users/nidhi/Documents/Projects/VTHACK/vthacks_2026/
        app/vthacks-career-app/public/collage/

  4. Refresh http://localhost:3000 — they appear. No code change needed.

  NAMING MATTERS. Lowercase, two digits, .jpg:
        story-01.jpg   NOT story-1.jpg, Story-01.jpg, or story-01.png

  SIZE: portrait or square, 1200px or larger on the short edge. The frame is
  tall and crops to fill, so a portrait image wastes least.

  SHORT ON TIME? Do 01, 03, 07, 08 only. Exhaustion -> silence -> the moment of
  doubt -> verification. Those four carry the whole argument, and 07 and 08 are
  the two the GoDaddy judges care about. The other six can stay empty frames
  without the story breaking.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT EACH CARD SAYS ON SCREEN (the words are already built, images only)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  01  THE PROBLEM    Another hundred applications.
                     Every day. Most of them never answered.
  02  THE PROBLEM    The same details. Every single site.
                     Name, education, experience, upload the same PDF again.
  03  THE PROBLEM    Hours gone. Nothing back.
                     Repetitive work that leads nowhere, and no way to tell
                     what worked.
  04  THE TURN       So we built HireWire.
                     One agent that does the applying, and refuses when
                     something is wrong.
  05  HOW IT WORKS   Just talk to it.
                     Say what you are looking for. No forms, no mouse, no tabs.
  06  HOW IT WORKS   Your resume in, the right jobs out.
                     Add your resume and LinkedIn once. Get roles matched to
                     you, not keywords.
  07  HOW IT WORKS   Wait — is this employer real?
                     Before anything is sent, it asks the question you never
                     get to ask.
  08  HOW IT WORKS   Our agent asks theirs to prove it.
                     Only once the employer is verified does your resume leave
                     your hands.
  09  HOW IT WORKS   Hiring? It runs both ways.
                     The employer agent verifies the applicant too. No bots at
                     the door.
  10  THE FUTURE     Agents talk. You decide.
                     The repetitive part disappears. You are brought in only
                     when it matters.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IF SOMETHING LOOKS WRONG AFTER YOU DROP THEM IN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Image does not appear    Check the filename character by character. It is the
                           cause almost every time.
  Appears on some cards    The missing ones are named wrong, or are .png.
  Badly cropped            The frame is tall and crops from the centre. Use a
                           portrait or square source, not a wide landscape one.
  Still the old page       Hard refresh: Cmd + Shift + R.
